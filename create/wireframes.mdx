*Explore layouts quickly before you commit to visual design.*

Wireframes help you agree on structure and content before colour, type and components come in. Use them early, when you want to compare a few layouts or get feedback without the distraction of polish.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe the screen">
    Tell Figr what the screen is for and what it needs to show. For example: "Wireframe a few layout options for an order tracking screen."

    <Tip>
      Keep wireframe prompts about structure and content, not style. Save colour and visual feedback for the high-fidelity version.
    </Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your context">
    Click **Add context** in the chat box and add screenshots, a [walkthrough recording](/input-research/screen-recording) or [Figma frames](/input-research/figma-input), so the wireframe reflects your product's real content and structure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the wireframe on the canvas">
    Figr creates a low-fidelity layout you can scan in seconds.

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="Low-fidelity wireframe of an order tracking screen on the Figr canvas"
        title="A wireframe generated by Figr"
        src="/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-3.22.52-PM.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask for alternatives">
    Request a different layout to compare side by side. For example: "Show two more layout options with the delivery timeline as the main focus."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn the one you like into a design">
    Pick your design system in the chat box and ask Figr to turn the wireframe into high-fidelity screens, adding any missing screens and states.

    <Frame>
      <img

        title="Turn a wireframe into a high-fidelity design"
        src="/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-3.24.12-PM.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
</Steps>
