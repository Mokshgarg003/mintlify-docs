*Explore layouts quickly before you commit to visual design.*

Wireframes help you agree on structure and content before colour, type and components come in. Use them early, when you want to compare a few layouts or get feedback without the distraction of polish.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe the screen">
    Tell Figr what the screen is for and what it needs to show. For example: "Wireframe a project dashboard with recent activity, open tasks and a team list."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach your Context Pod">
    Select your pod in the chat so the wireframe reflects your product's real content and structure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the wireframe on the canvas">
    Figr creates a low-fidelity layout you can scan in seconds.

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="A wireframe on the canvas"
        src="/images/docs/wf-03-wireframe-1.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask for alternatives">
    Request a different layout to compare side by side. For example: "Show two more layout options with the task list as the main focus."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn the one you like into a design">
    Attach your design system and ask Figr to turn the wireframe into high-fidelity prototyped screens and add missing screens and states.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Keep wireframe prompts about structure and content, not style. Save colour and visual feedback for the high-fidelity version.
</Tip>
