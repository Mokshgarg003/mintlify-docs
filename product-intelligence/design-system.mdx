With your design system in Figr, every screen uses your real colours, type and components from the first message. You spend less time fixing off-brand drafts and more time on the design itself.

A design system can pull from several sources at once: Figma, Storybook, an npm package, a GitHub repo, uploaded code files, connectors and brand assets.

## Create a design system

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a new design system">
    In the sidebar, open **Design System** and create a new one. Give it a name and choose who can use it: just you, everyone in your workspace to view, or everyone to edit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add one or more sources">
    Under **Sources**, pick where your design system lives. See the options below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set it up">
    Click **Setup design system**. Figr reads your sources and builds the design system, with your tokens, components, assets and guidance on when to use each component.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Choose your sources

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Figma">
    Bring in your Figma variables, styles and components with the **Figr AI Figma Sync** plugin.

    1. Under **Sources**, choose **Figma**, then click **Open Figma plugin**.
    2. Run **Figr AI Figma Sync** in your design system file and check it shows **Connected**.
    3. Select what Figr should learn from: variables, styles, fonts, components, reference screens, icons, logos and images.
    4. Click **Setup design system**.

    <Note>
      Your colours, type and spacing need to be set up as variables or styles in Figma. Figr can't pick up values that only live on individual layers.
    </Note>

    Include a few reference screens with your components. They show Figr how components are used together, not just what they look like.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Storybook">
    Point Figr at your published Storybook, so it designs with the components your engineers already ship.

    1. Under **Sources**, choose **Storybook**.
    2. Paste the URL of your published Storybook.
    3. Click **Setup design system**. Figr reads your components, their variants and states.

    Make sure each component has stories for its main variants and states, like default, hover, disabled and error. Figr can only use what your stories show.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="npm package">
    Use your published component library directly.

    1. Under **Sources**, choose **npm package**.
    2. Enter the package name, like `@yourcompany/design-system`, and the version you want.
    3. Click **Setup design system**.

    When you publish a new version of your package, update the version in Figr so new designs use your latest components.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="GitHub or code files">
    Build your design system from your codebase, so designs line up with what's actually built.

    1. Under **Sources**, choose **GitHub**, select the repo, and the folder where your design system lives if it isn't at the root.
    2. Click **Setup design system**.

    Not on GitHub? Upload your theme or style files, like CSS or token files, as a source instead.

    Point Figr at the folder that holds your tokens and shared components, not the whole app. A focused source gives cleaner results.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Brand assets">
    No full design system yet? A few brand assets are enough for Figr to match your look.

    1. Under **Sources**, choose **Brand assets**.
    2. Upload your logo, brand colours, fonts, icons and a few screenshots of your product or site. Add a short note for any rules, like "Use our primary blue only for buttons."
    3. Click **Setup design system**.

    Five screenshots give Figr a basic read of your style. Around 20 give it a much deeper one.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Tip>
  No design system attached at all? Figr can pick up your colours, fonts and logo from your website automatically.
</Tip>

## Use it in a project

Pick a design system when you create a canvas, or switch at any time from the chat box.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the design system picker">
    In the chat box, click the design system chip to see every design system you can use.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the one you want">
    Search by name. Your workspace default and most-used design systems are marked so they're easy to spot.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select it and send">
    Figr uses the selected design system for your next message and the ones after it, until you switch again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Want the same screen in two styles? Ask for it once, switch design systems, then ask again. You'll get both side by side on the canvas.

## Set a default for your workspace

Admins can set a default design system that new projects attach automatically. If there's no default, Figr recommends the most-used one in the picker.

## Hand off with your design system

- **Export to Figma.** Elements that match your Figma library export as instances of your components. See [Export to Figma](/export/figma).
- **Coding agents.** Agents connected through [Figr MCP](/export/mcp) can read your components and tokens and build against them.

## Why it matters

| Without a design system | With your design system |
| --- | --- |
| Generic components that need restyling | Your actual components with the right variants |
| Colours that miss your brand | Your colour tokens, applied automatically |
| Spacing that feels off | Your spacing scale throughout |
| Typography that needs fixing | Your type hierarchy respected |
