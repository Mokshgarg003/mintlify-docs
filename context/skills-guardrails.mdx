Skills are instructions Figr follows while it designs. Use them for compliance rules, accessibility standards, anti-dark-pattern guidelines, or your team's own way of working. Skills apply across your organization, so every project follows them, whoever is designing.

## Always on or when relevant

Each skill has a setting that decides when Figr uses it.

- **Always on:** Figr applies the skill to every request. Use it for rules that never bend.
  - "All text must meet WCAG AA contrast."
  - "Never pre-select consent or marketing checkboxes."
  - "Show fees before the confirm button, never after."
- **When relevant:** Figr applies the skill only when the request matches it. Use it for rules that belong to one area of your product.
  - "When designing checkout, show the order total on every step."
  - "When designing onboarding, keep it to three steps or fewer."
  - "When designing for mobile, use bottom sheets instead of modals."

## Create a skill

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Skills and create a new skill">
    Give it a clear name, like **Payments compliance**, and a short description of what it covers.

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="New skill form in Figr with a name and description"
        title="Create a skill in Figr"
        src="/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-2.41.58-AM.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the rules">
    List each rule as a short, specific statement Figr can check a design against.

    <Tip>
      Write rules you could check with a yes or no. "Fees are visible before confirm" works better than "be transparent about fees".
    </Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose when Figr uses it">
    Under **When should Figr use this?**, pick **Always on** or **When relevant**.

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="When should Figr use this setting with Always on and When relevant options"
        title="Choose when a Figr skill applies"
        src="/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-2.42.47-AM.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save it">
    Save the skill. It applies to projects across your organization from then on.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Example: anti-dark-pattern rules for payments

A fintech team wanted every payment flow to be clear and honest, no matter who designed it. They added these rules as an **Always on** skill:

- Payment details, fees and exchange rates always have strong visual hierarchy. They are never hidden behind a toggle, an accordion or small text.
- Every payment has an explicit confirmation step that shows the final amount.
- Toggles and defaults never pre-select the option that costs the user more.
