Skills are instructions Figr follows while it designs. Use them for compliance rules, accessibility standards, anti-dark-pattern guidelines, or your team’s own way of working. Turn a skill on for your whole organisation and every project follows it, whoever is designing.

## Always on or when relevant

Each skill has a mode that decides when Figr uses it.

- **Always on:** Figr applies the skill to every request. Use it for rules that never bend.
  - “All text must meet WCAG AA contrast.”
  - “Never pre-select consent or marketing checkboxes.”
  - “Show fees before the confirm button, never after.”
- **When relevant:** Figr applies the skill only when the request matches it. Use it for rules that belong to one area of your product.
  - “When designing checkout, show the order total on every step.”
  - “When designing onboarding, keep it to three steps or fewer.”
  - “When designing for mobile, use bottom sheets instead of modals.”

## Create a skill

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Skills and create a new skill">
    Give it a clear name, like **Payments compliance**, and a short description of what it covers.

    ![Screenshot 2026 10 21 At 2 41 58 AM](/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-2.41.58-AM.png "Screenshot 2026 10 21 At 2 41 58 AM")

    {/* Screenshot: new skill form with name and description */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the rules">
    List each rule as a short, specific statement Figr can check a design against.

    {/* Screenshot: skill rules being written */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the mode">
    Pick **Always on** or **When relevant**.

    ![Screenshot 2026 10 21 At 2 42 47 AM](/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-2.42.47-AM.png "Screenshot 2026 10 21 At 2 42 47 AM")

    {/* Screenshot: mode selector showing Always on and When relevant */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn it on for your organisation">
    Enable the skill org-wide so it applies to every project and every teammate.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Write rules you could check with a yes or no. “Fees are visible before confirm” works better than “be transparent about fees”.
</Tip>

## Example: anti-dark-pattern rules for payments

A fintech team wanted every payment flow to be clear and honest, no matter who designed it. They added these rules as an **Always on** skill, enabled for the whole organisation:

- Payment details, fees and exchange rates always have strong visual hierarchy. They are never hidden behind a toggle, an accordion or small text.
- Every payment has an explicit confirmation step that shows the final amount.
- Toggles and defaults never pre-select the option that costs the user more.

![Image](/images/image-32.png "Image")

Now every design Figr creates for the team follows these rules, without anyone having to repeat them in a prompt.
