_Lay out your canvas so the work tells a story._

Everything Figr creates sits on the canvas as a node: screens, flows, docs, wireframes and the files you add. Move them around and connect them so anyone opening the project can follow how one piece leads to the next.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Move nodes into place">
    Drag any node to where you want it. Put related work next to each other, like a PRD beside the screens it describes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect related nodes">
    Draw a connector from one node to another to show how they relate, such as a flow leading to its screens. Figr reads these connections as context on its next turn.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tidy the layout">
    Put related nodes in a section, and align or space them evenly so the canvas is easy to scan.

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="Canvas organized with sections, notes and connector lines"
        src="/images/Canvas-Organize.avif"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  **In a hurry?** Use **Organize canvas** to group nodes automatically by creation date or by type. Sections you made yourself stay as they are.
</Info>

<Tip>
  Lay the canvas out left to right in the order the work happened: context, then flow, then screens, then docs. New teammates can read it like a timeline.
</Tip>
