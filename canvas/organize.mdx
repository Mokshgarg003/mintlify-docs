_Keep your canvas tidy, readable and easy to reuse._

Everything Figr creates sits on the canvas as a node: screens, flows, docs, wireframes and the files you add. Group related work, connect nodes to show how they relate, and let Figr group the canvas for you when it gets busy.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Move nodes into place">
    Drag any node to where you want it. Put related work next to each other, like a PRD beside the screens it describes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect related nodes">
    Draw a connector from one node to another to show how they relate, such as a flow leading to its screens. Figr reads these connections as context on its next turn.

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="Two nodes on the canvas joined by a connector line"
        src="/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-3.46.19-PM.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Group related nodes into sections">
    Put related nodes in a section, like "Onboarding v2" or "Research", so they stay together and move as one.

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="Related nodes grouped inside a named section"
        src="/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-3.16.16-AM.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Organize the canvas automatically">
    When the canvas gets busy, open the **Layers** panel and use **Organize canvas**. Figr groups your nodes into sections by creation date or by type. Sections you made yourself stay as they are.

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="Organize canvas option in the Layers panel"
        src="/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-3.15.06-AM.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Lay the canvas out left to right in the order the work happened: context, then flow, then screens, then docs. New teammates can read it like a timeline.
</Tip>
