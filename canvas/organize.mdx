_Keep your canvas tidy, readable and easy to reuse._

Everything Figr creates sits on the canvas as a node: screens, flows, docs, wireframes and the files you add. Clean up the layout in one click, group related work, connect nodes to show how they relate, and copy them into other canvases when you need them again.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Move nodes into place">
    Drag any node to where you want it. Put related work next to each other, like a PRD beside the screens it describes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect related nodes">
    Draw a connector from one node to another to show how they relate, such as a flow leading to its screens. Figr reads these connections as context on its next turn.

    <Frame>
      ![Screenshot 2026 10 21 At 3 46 19 PM](/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-3.46.19-PM.png "Screenshot 2026 10 21 At 3 46 19 PM")
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply Auto-clean up">
    When the canvas gets busy, use **Organize canvas** to tidy it automatically. Figr lines up your nodes, spaces them evenly and removes overlaps, so the canvas is easy to scan again

    <Info>
      **In a hurry?** Use **Organize canvas** to group nodes automatically by creation date or by type. Sections you made yourself stay as they are.
    </Info>

    ![Screenshot 2026 10 21 At 3 15 06 AM](/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-3.15.06-AM.png "Screenshot 2026 10 21 At 3 15 06 AM")
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Group nodes into sections

Put related nodes in a section, like "Onboarding v2" or "Research", so they stay together and move as one. See _Group work into sections_ for the full steps. 

![Screenshot 2026 10 21 At 3 16 16 AM](/images/Screenshot-2026-10-21-at-3.16.16-AM.png "Screenshot 2026 10 21 At 3 16 16 AM")

<Tip>
  Lay the canvas out left to right in the order the work happened: context, then flow, then screens, then docs. New teammates can read it like a timeline.
</Tip>
