*Explain your design decisions right on the canvas.*

<iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/ZZHGlkyyJgc" title="Figr Canvas: show, point, annotate, then design" frameBorder="0" className="w-full aspect-video rounded-xl" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowFullScreen />

Annotations are notes that stay visible next to your work. Use them to explain why a design looks the way it does, call out behaviour that isn't obvious from a static screen, or leave notes for developers before handoff. Unlike comments, annotations aren't conversations and don't get resolved.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add an annotation">
    Choose the **Sticky Note** tool in the canvas toolbar and click where you want the note to appear.

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="The Sticky Note tool in the canvas toolbar"
        src="/images/docs/ann-01-tool.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the note">
    Keep it short and specific. For example: "Button stays disabled until all required fields are filled."

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="A sticky note on the canvas with its formatting toolbar"
        src="/images/docs/ann-02-note.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Point it at what it describes">
    Place the annotation beside the screen or element it refers to, or connect it with a line so it's clear what it's about.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update it as the design changes">
    Edit or delete annotations when the design moves on, so they never describe an old version.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  Figr reads annotations on its next turn, so a note on the canvas also guides the next design.
</Info>

<Tip>
  Annotate the things a developer would otherwise have to ask about: what happens on hover, loading, errors and empty states.
</Tip>
