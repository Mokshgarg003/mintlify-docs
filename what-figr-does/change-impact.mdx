Small changes rarely stay small. Teams have several people working on the same payments page, each with their own focus. Nobody sees the conflicts until development, when they cost the most to fix.

Figr shows you everything a change touches before anyone scopes it.

<Frame>
  <img

</Frame>

<Tip>
  **The short version:** Describe a change, like a new option or a new rule. Figr traces it across every screen and flow in your product that it affects, and shows what needs to change on each, so conflicts surface in planning instead of in development.
</Tip>

**Example on this page:** FreshFold, a laundry pickup and delivery app. Customers book a pickup in the app, a rider collects the bag, and clean clothes come back within 24 hours.

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe the change">
    One line is enough. In the FreshFold example: adding express 6-hour delivery as an option on every order.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Figr checks what it knows about your product">
    Figr uses what it knows about your product, from your [Context Pod](/product-intelligence/context-pods) and the context you've added, to find every place the change shows up.

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="Express delivery change request in the Figr chat box with product context attached"
        title="Describe a product change to Figr"
        src="/images/image-20.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Figr maps the impact">
    You get every affected screen, with what needs to change on each and where changes overlap.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Figr designs the updates">
    Ask Figr to update the affected screens, and it designs them together so they stay consistent.

    <Frame>
      <img
        alt="Affected screens updated together for express delivery on the Figr canvas"
        title="Design every affected screen together"
        src="/images/image-21.png"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What changes for your team

- **PMs** scope with the full picture, not a guess.
- **Several PMs on one area** see where their changes overlap while they plan.
- **Engineers** stop finding missed screens mid-sprint.
