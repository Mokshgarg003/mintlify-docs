Small changes rarely stay small. One team had three PMs working on the same payments page, each with their own change. Nobody saw the conflicts until development, when they cost the most to fix.

Figr shows you everything a change touches before anyone scopes it.

<Note>
  **The short version:** Describe a change, like a new field or a new rule. Figr traces it across every screen and flow in your product that it affects, and shows what needs to change on each, so conflicts surface in planning instead of in development.
</Note>

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe the change">
    One line is enough. In the Ledgerly example: adding a required cost center field to every expense.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Figr checks what it knows about your product">
    Figr uses your Context Pod, the product knowledge it keeps for your team, to find every place the change shows up.

    <Info>
      **Asset needed · 3.1**: Screenshot of the Ledgerly Context Pod being used in the chat, with the prompt and the pod chip visible in the composer.
    </Info>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Figr maps the impact">
    You get every affected screen and component, side by side, with what needs to change on each and where changes overlap.

    <Info>
      **Asset needed · 3.2**: Screenshot of the impact map on the canvas: the cost center change linked to five or six Ledgerly screens (submit expense, approvals, reports, export, admin settings), with notes on each.
    </Info>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Figr designs the updates">
    Ask Figr to update the affected screens, and it designs them together so they stay consistent.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## See it work

The moment that matters: one field change, traced to every screen it touches, before a single ticket is written.

<Info>
  **Asset needed · 3.3 (proof moment)**: 20–30s video. The cost center prompt is sent, and the affected Ledgerly screens appear one by one on the canvas, linked to the change, ending on the full map.
</Info>

## What changes for your team

- **PMs** scope with the full picture, not a guess.
- **Several PMs on one area** see each other's changes before they collide.
- **Engineers** stop finding missed screens mid-sprint.
