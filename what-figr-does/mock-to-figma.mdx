PMs can now mock up an idea in minutes. Then a designer spends a day or two rebuilding it in Figma so the team can actually use it. Two product teams told us this rebuild cost them one to two days on every handoff.

Figr removes the rebuild. The mock goes to Figma as real, editable design.

<Note>
  **The short version:** A PM brings a rough mock, sketch or screenshot. Figr turns it into a prototype in your design system, and one click sends it to Figma as editable layers with your variables attached. Designers refine instead of redraw.
</Note>

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Bring the mock">
    Upload a screenshot, a sketch or an AI-generated mock. In the Ledgerly example: a PM's rough mock of a new approvals screen.

    <Info>
      **Asset needed · 6.1**: The PM's rough Ledgerly approvals mock (screenshot or sketch) attached in the composer.
    </Info>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Figr rebuilds it in your system">
    Figr turns the mock into a prototype that uses your tokens and components, not the mock's one-off styles.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Export to Figma">
    Select the prototype and choose **Export to Figma**. It runs through the Figr Figma plugin you already use for Figma input.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick up in Figma">
    The design lands in your Figma file as editable layers with your variables attached, ready for a designer to refine and hand off.

    <Info>
      **Asset needed · 6.2**: Screenshot of the Ledgerly approvals screen in Figma with the layers panel and variables panel open.
    </Info>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What changes for your team

- **PMs** share ideas designers can use, not ideas they have to redraw.
- **Designers** get back the days spent rebuilding.
- **Handoffs** keep your variables, so the design stays on system.
