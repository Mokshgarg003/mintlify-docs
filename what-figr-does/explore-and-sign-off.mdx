Good design decisions need options. One team wanted three or four directions for every idea before committing. Another couldn't trace why a design ended up the way it did, because the decisions lived in meetings and chat threads.

Figr gives you the options fast and keeps the decisions next to the work.

<Note>
  **The short version:** Ask Figr for several directions at once and compare them side by side. Share one link for review, collect comments on the design itself, and have Figr act on the feedback, with every decision kept on the canvas.
</Note>

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask for directions">
    Describe the idea and how many directions you want. In the Ledgerly example: three takes on the spend dashboard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare them side by side">
    Each direction sits on the canvas next to the others. Take any one further in its own thread without losing the rest.

    <Info>
      **Asset needed · 5.1**: Screenshot of three Ledgerly spend-dashboard directions side by side on the canvas (dense for finance, simple for managers, mobile-first), labelled.
    </Info>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share one link for review">
    Send stakeholders a link. They comment on the design itself, not in a separate thread.

    <Info>
      **Asset needed · 5.2**: Screenshot of the shared review link with two or three comments pinned on the chosen Ledgerly direction.
    </Info>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Figr acts on the feedback">
    Ask Figr to apply a comment, and it updates the design. The comment, the change and the reason stay together.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The moment that matters: from three directions to one signed-off design, with every decision visible on the canvas.

## What changes for your team

- **Designers** explore more directions in the time one used to take.
- **Stakeholders** review the design itself, from one link.
- **Everyone** can see why a decision was made, months later.
