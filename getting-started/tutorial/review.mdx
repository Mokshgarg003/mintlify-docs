You don't need a meeting or a senior reviewer on call. Point Figr at a screen, flow or prototype and it returns a prioritized list of UX issues, each pinned to the exact spot and paired with a concrete fix.

## Open a review

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask for a review">
    Attach a screenshot, recording, Figma frames or a Figr Capture, or point at a prototype on your canvas. Ask Figr to review it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the review card">
    When Figr finishes, it posts a review card in the chat. Hover the card and click **View Review**.

    <Frame caption="Hover the review card in chat and click View Review.">
      ![Review card in chat with the View Review button](/images/03-04-review-ux-\(1\).png)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Work through the issues">
    Every issue is pinned to its spot on the screen and ranked by priority. Click a pin or a list item to read the issue and the suggested fix.

    <Frame caption="Each issue is pinned on the screen, with the issue and a suggested fix.">
      ![Design review panel with prioritized, pinned UX issues](/images/03-04-review-ux.png)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What Figr flags

- Friction points and places the flow might break
- Unclear hierarchy and confusing copy
- Missing states
- Accessibility risks
- Product logic gaps and patterns that miss user intent

## Example prompts

- "Review this screen for UX friction and prioritize the top issues."
- "Audit this onboarding flow and identify where users may drop off."
- "Review this flow for missing states, unclear hierarchy and accessibility risks."
- "Compare this screen with the competitor reference and explain the trade-offs."

## Review first, redesign later

A review is a low-risk place to start. You're not asking Figr to build anything, so you decide what changes. When you're ready, ask Figr to redesign the screen and fix the top issues. Attach a [Context Pod](/product-intelligence/context-pods) so the review judges the screen against how your product actually works.
