A prototype turns a clear product direction into working, high-fidelity screens in your design system. Because Figr holds your product context and design system, the output uses your existing patterns, components, spacing and typography. It's real front-end code, not a mockup you have to clean up.

## Build a prototype

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add your context">
    Attach your Context Pod and design system, plus any screenshots, recordings or Figma frames that show where this fits in your product.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe what to build">
    Say who it's for, what they need to do and where it fits. Figr may ask a few questions first. Answer them, or tell it to go ahead with its best guess.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click through it">
    Select the prototype and click **Interact** to try it on the canvas, or **Full screen** to see it at device size.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check every state">
    Switch to the **Screens** view to see every route and state on one board. Use **Generate screens** to build the states that are missing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Refine it">
    Ask for changes in chat, or click **Edit** to change one part without regenerating the rest. See [Edit without regenerating](/getting-started/tutorial/untitled-page-5).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you get

- **React or Angular.** Prototypes are built in React by default, or Angular with Angular Material, with the same design system support.
- **Your design system.** Components and tokens come from the design system you attach.
- **Theme preview.** Preview supported prototypes in System, Light or Dark mode from full screen.
- **Versions.** Every prototype keeps its own version history.

## Good prompts

- "Design a customer success dashboard showing account health, open risks, upcoming renewals and expansion opportunities. Use the attached design system and product context."
- "Generate a billing settings page with plan details, invoices, payment method and team usage. Follow the existing settings layout and component patterns."
- "Create the empty, loading and error states for this activity feed."

Build a prototype when the problem is clear, the key flow is mapped and the important states are known. If they aren't, [brainstorm](/getting-started/tutorial/brainstorm) or [map the flow](/getting-started/tutorial/map-edge-cases) first.
