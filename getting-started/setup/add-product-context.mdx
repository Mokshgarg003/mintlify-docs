Your first setup decides the quality of everything Figr generates. Give it real product context, how your product works and how it looks, before you ask for a single screen.

## 1. Set up a Context Pod

A Context Pod teaches Figr how your product works.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the pod">
    In the sidebar, open **Product intelligence** and click **Create new Context Pod**. Name it after one product area, like "Billing and plans", "Admin permissions" or "Workspace onboarding".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a walkthrough">
    Under **Show us your product**, record a walkthrough or drop in an existing recording. Explain the area like you would to a new teammate: what it does, who uses it, what they're trying to get done, where the friction is, and what shouldn't change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add anything else you have">
    Upload PRDs or research, paste notes, link a public page, or add instructions like the terms your team uses.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build it">
    Click **Start Building**. Figr reads your sources and shows a summary of what it learned.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Keep each pod focused on one product area, workflow or feature set. A recording beats a screenshot, because Figr follows the real sequence of clicks, menus and transitions. See [Context Pods](/product-intelligence/context-pods) for the details.

## 2. Set up your design system

This is where Figr learns how your product looks. Open **Design System** in the sidebar and add a source: Figma (through the Figr AI Figma Sync plugin), Storybook, an npm package, a GitHub repo, code files or brand assets. No design system yet? Start from brand assets, or let Figr pick up colours and fonts from your website.

## 3. Connect your tools (optional)

Connect Notion, Confluence, Linear, Asana, GitHub, Slack, Mixpanel and others so Figr can read live specs, tickets and data while it works. See [Connectors](/product-intelligence/connectors).

## 4. Start a canvas with your context

Open your pod and start a canvas from it, or click **New canvas** and pick your Context Pod and design system. Your first message already has your product's context.

## Start with lighter tasks

Don't jump straight to high fidelity. Start with a review, a flow, an edge-case map or a PRD breakdown, so Figr reasons through the problem before it builds screens:

- "Review this onboarding flow and identify where users may get stuck."
- "Map the edge cases for this billing upgrade flow."
- "Create a user flow from this product walkthrough."
- "Turn this rough PRD into a flow and key states."

Generate the design once the direction is clear. By then Figr knows how your product looks and behaves, and the area you're working in.
